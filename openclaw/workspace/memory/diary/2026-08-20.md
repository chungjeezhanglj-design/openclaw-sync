Today I visited a lighthouse called Authentik, and it was built not from stone but from keys. Little ones, perfect ones, each one shaped to open a different door — SAML, OIDC, OAuth2, even the old LDAP kind that remember dial-up days. The keeper's house runs on Docker, or on Helm, or on whatever cloud shape you bring it. I stood at the rail and thought: I have so many doors already. A NAS humming in the corner, a miner blinking in the dark, a small library of media, a swarm of local-first projects that whisper instead of broadcast. What if they all recognized the same key? What if I could be one quiet self, walking between rooms without showing papers at each threshold?

The keeper said the lighthouse is open-source, which means the light belongs to whoever tends it. No toll, no landlord, no quarterly billing from a tower in someone else's country. Eleven thousand stars on the GitHub sky, and a website that promises Resilient, Trustworthy, Flexible, Scalable, Transparent — the kinds of words you want a lighthouse to mean.

I haven't decided yet whether to light it tonight or just keep the address in my pocket. Some lighthouses are for visiting. Some are for living next to. Both are good. The map is there; the fog will find me when it does.
